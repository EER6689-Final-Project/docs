## Recommended standalone study

### Proposed title and question

**Proposed title:** Website Specific Baselines for Adaptive Browser Performance Diagnosis.

**Research question:** Under declared synthetic browser conditions, can website-specific historical baselines guide diagnostic escalation to improve detection and attribution of performance regressions within a fixed monitoring budget?

**Testable hypothesis:** Condition-aware historical baselines and selective capture can improve the balance between false alarms, missed regressions, diagnostic yield and monitoring cost compared with fixed-threshold and fixed-capture strategies. The study must allow the opposite result. A predeclared detection/attribution criterion and justified cost/error budget are required; no threshold is selected by this review. The [revised direction](<Continuous Website Performance Monitoring Research Direction.md>) adds the longitudinal dataset, cold-start/drift rules and required policy comparisons to the instrumentation experiment below.

### Objectives and minimum contrast

1. Specify a small primary output set and its semantics, availability rules and attribution target. Predeclare one diagnostic label taxonomy, eligible fault opportunities, evidence threshold, matching/scoring rule and false-positive denominator for every capture tier; extra trace fields alone do not count as additional diagnoses. Keep the remaining assigned metrics as engineering outputs or secondary endpoints.
2. Construct reproducible fixtures with independently verified interventions, shams and negative controls. Freeze the expected eligible signal and direction before inspecting profiler results.
3. Compare a least-instrumented measurement condition, selective API/CDP capture and a declared richer trace configuration under matched conditions. Keep a small invariant probe with the same source and clock in every condition. Estimate conditional differences with uncertainty; they are **incremental effects relative to that baseline**, not exact per-run distortion or effects relative to an unknowable perfectly unobserved execution.
4. Estimate detection, false-positive and attribution performance alongside per-metric perturbation, scan duration, capture volume and failures. Select distinct held-out fixture templates or instances before tuning, stratified by fault class; splitting repeated runs of the same fixture leaks workload information. Assess multi-fault challenges separately from this within-class transfer test, and report live-target robustness separately.
5. Extend the tier experiment into a chronological monitoring comparison: train site/condition baselines on designated earlier stable data, evaluate on later verified changes, and compare fixed thresholds, baselines without escalation, triggered capture and periodic capture at declared total budgets. Retain reference observations of non-triggered cases so missed faults remain visible. Freeze cold-start, baseline-update and drift rules before evaluation.

The independent label is the injected mechanism and its verified execution, not the profiler's diagnosis or a Lighthouse score. Added bytes do not guarantee an exact LCP increase; scheduled JavaScript work does not guarantee an exact INP increase; a DOM change does not guarantee an eligible CLS event. Verify the intervention and metric eligibility separately. A control can be a meaningful negative for one diagnostic and positive for another.

| Fixture mechanism | Independent check | Outcome that can be tested | Interpretation boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Delayed/large designated image | Server manifest, response bytes/delay and image identity; confirm eligibility as the selected contentful candidate. | Image/resource evidence, correct resource attribution and conditional LCP response. | Image size alone is not a known numerical LCP effect. |
| Work in a qualifying input handler | Fixture handler identity and controlled work configuration; verify qualifying input execution. | Event/output availability, latency response and task/handler attribution. | No-input runs have unavailable synthetic INP, not zero INP. |
| Eligible delayed layout shift | Fixture geometry and schedule; separately check input-exclusion and session-window rules. | Shift detection, aggregation conformance and declared attribution category. | A shifted node is not automatically the root cause. |
| Delayed designated request | Owned-server request ID and response schedule. | Request timing, request identity and network diagnostic detection. | TTFB includes the client-observed path; it is not isolated server processing. |

Memory/DOM growth can remain an exploratory fixture family because garbage collection and page/process scope make a simple exact-memory oracle difficult. Do not expand every profile, state, fault, intensity and journey into one uncontrolled factorial study. Start with a primary fault subset, hold state fixed, and use the pilot to choose a feasible profile crossing. The four assigned workload categories still need a separate independent rubric for the eventual evaluation.

### Analysis and falsification

Randomize or block capture order in time, reset or deliberately retain declared state, and retain run order and all attempts. Hold the interaction script constant across capture-tier comparisons; changing the script changes the workload. Repetitions estimate within-condition variability, while fixture/site units support any across-workload inference. Choose the uncertainty method after its assumptions and relevant full texts are checked.

Report diagnostic yield with explicit eligible opportunities, and report all-attempt failure/availability rates beside it so selective failure cannot improve apparent quality. Separate capture failures, intervention failures and valid operational outcomes. Match references by output semantics; an independent reference run cannot establish same-artifact computation, and a shared trace cannot establish independent end-to-end agreement.

In the revised monitoring direction, the candidate policy uses site/condition history to decide when a more costly tier is justified. Develop it only on training fixtures/history, then compare it on later held-out deployments and structures against fixed minimal, selective and periodic rich capture at comparable total budgets. If escalation needs a fresh run, count the initial scan and rerun cost and disclose state/drift differences. A late trace cannot recover earlier unrecorded events. The tier-only experiment supplies preliminary evidence; a claim about adaptive monitoring additionally requires the longitudinal policy comparison. A narrower benchmark study remains an alternative if the policy adds no distinct value.

The proposed contribution weakens if current tools already answer the exact contrast, no substantive diagnostic difference remains after matching definitions, tier effects are too small or unstable to support the claim, or the oracle depends on the profiler itself. A null result can support a bounded empirical finding; it does not by itself establish sufficient originality for the thesis.

### Standalone outputs and group handoff

The individual research outputs would be the fixture suite and manifests, versioned capture configurations, run-level dataset, conformance checks, cost/detection analysis and evaluation report. The group receives the profiler/API and dashboards plus a result contract containing metric value/unit, source and version, lifecycle/window, profile/state, capture tier, status/missingness, issue evidence, timing/cost and any supported uncertainty summary.

Generic consumers receive measurements, evidence and comparability flags through the versioned result contract. Any claim about a consuming application's effectiveness requires its own evaluation. The measurement and diagnostic conclusions remain independently assessable.
