# Pilot repetition and finite horizon notes (Luna)

## Source findings (not universal browser run-count rules)

1. **Kalibera & Jones (2013), “Rigorous Benchmarking in Reasonable Time,” *ACM SIGPLAN Notices* 48(11), pp. 63–74.** The locally inspected ACM typeset published version gives DOI 10.1145/2555670.2464160; the Kent accepted-manuscript record identifies ISMM ’13 and DOI 10.1145/2464157.2464160. The project note flags this cross-version DOI metadata discrepancy; cite the version used and do not imply separate studies. KAR accepted manuscript: https://kar.kent.ac.uk/33611/45/p63-kaliber.pdf ; the project also holds the ACM typeset published version, *ACM SIGPLAN Notices* 48(11), pp. 63–74. In the locally inspected published version, see §§3–4 (PDF pp. 2–3) for variation levels/design; §9, especially §9.3 (PDF p. 9), for multi-level allocation and confidence intervals; §10 (PDF p. 9) for speedup; §11 (PDF pp. 10–11) for configuration-specific repetition counts; §12 (PDF p. 11) for summary and caution against transferring counts. The accepted manuscript and published version are materially the same work but section numbering/pagination should be cited to the version actually read. The paper does not prescribe 20/30 as browser defaults; any initial dimensioning count is a starting point within its benchmark-specific methodology. Native VM/compiler benchmark outcomes, not browser trace or cause-attribution outcomes.

2. **Heričko, Šumak & Brdnik (2021), “Towards Representative Web Performance Measurements with Google Lighthouse,” Proceedings of the 2021 7th Student Computer Science Research Conference (StuCoSReC), pp. 39–42, University of Maribor Press, DOI 10.18690/978-961-286-516-0.9.** Primary PDF: https://www.scores.si/assets/pdf/scores2021.pdf . §2 (PDF p. 40; extracted lines 1863–68 in the scores.si PDF) review of eight prior Lighthouse studies found strategies ranging 1–100 runs; §3 (PDF pp. 40–41; extracted lines 1893–95 in the scores.si PDF) audits 10 Alexa sites four times at N=1,5,10,100 with Lighthouse 7.3.0 / Headless Chrome, macOS 10.15.7, one broadband setup during one 4-hour afternoon; §4–5 (PDF pp. 41–42; extracted lines 1913–26, 2227–64 in the scores.si PDF) reports outliers at N=100 and no detected median difference for N=5 vs 10 vs 100 in nine of ten sites; authors recommend five consecutive runs aggregated by median. Caveat: this supports a cheap robustness check for Lighthouse scores in their specific setup. The metric is a composite score on real sites, not browser fault diagnosis, trace coverage, intermittent-fault attribution, independent days/sessions, or a power analysis. Their “reasonable time” claim is not an evaluated universal cost curve.

3. **Duplyakin et al. (2023), “Avoiding the Ordering Trap in Systems Performance Measurement,” USENIX ATC ’23.** Primary page/PDF: https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc23/presentation/duplyakin (author page: https://www.flux.utah.edu/paper/duplyakin-atc23). Abstract: trials may alter state and make order bias results; their empirical dataset is nearly 2.3M measurements from >1,700 servers over nine months. Applicability: supports checking and controlling browser/profile/cache/server state, blocking or randomizing arm order, and recording sequence. Not browser-specific and does not prescribe counts or prove fresh contexts eliminate all carryover.

4. **Netravali et al. (2018), “Vesper: Measuring Time-to-Interactivity for Web Pages,” NSDI ’18, pp. 217–231.** Official page: https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi18/presentation/netravali-vesper . Vesper defines Ready Index from functional interaction state and compares it to conventional page metrics. Useful mainly as a warning to fix the measurement target and interaction workload, not assume a generic page-load metric captures the fault. It does not supply a repetition-count recommendation, and its intervention/measurement setup is not the proposed delayed-diagnosis comparison.

## Operational pilot proposal (inference / choices to preregister)

- Make the pilot a **finite engineering and design pilot**, not a test of H1: freeze at 36 matched scenario blocks: 2 owned localhost fixtures × 3 fault classes with similar symptoms × 2 persistence patterns (persistent/intermittent) × 3 independently reinitialized fault occurrences. Within every block run the original-evidence policy C and delayed-follow-up policy B on separate original executions; B also gets its scheduled follow-up. Randomize B/C order by block, do not run them concurrently, and reset/document browser profile, cache, server fixture state, fault seed and interaction path. Three occurrences/cell is only a feasibility screen, far too few for a strong cell-level error-rate claim. If total pilot workload is too large, reduce the crossed factors (e.g., one fixture or two diagnostic classes) rather than claim that repeated page visits replace independent fault occurrences.
- Include stable controls and a deliberately misleading same-symptom replacement on follow-up. Verify ground truth in evaluator harness independently of policy alert/capture. Retain every assigned attempt, including failures, missing records, misses, abstentions, and cap exhaustion. Make C's original-visit evidence comparator real and useful: before extending the pilot, require it to capture at least one causal discriminator during the original interval, distinguish at least two injected causes in blinded examples, and export it under the fixed cap. Otherwise the central comparator has not been tested.
- Set a fixed horizon per block: one scheduled original visit, one B follow-up at the declared delay, and one terminal verification visit only if the protocol requires it. Do not keep visiting until a fault recurs or a diagnosis succeeds. Cap instrumentation/export bytes, elapsed time, memory, and CPU separately; log actual cost and all capture/export failures. Pilot results choose plausible cap levels and the main-study design; do not drop failures or tune thresholds on the final evaluation.
- Treat within-visit samples as subsamples, visits nested in fault occurrences, and fault occurrences nested in fixture/scenario. The main study's repetition count should be chosen from pilot variance/cluster structure and a target interval width or precision for the B–C difference in **all assigned episodes correctly attributed**; do not use Lighthouse's five-run result or Kalibera/Jones VM counts as the answer. Keep original/follow-up visit IDs and state provenance distinct. Because this is finite evaluation with scheduled opportunities, use a predeclared stopping rule based on the planned count/cap, not “stop once enough successful diagnoses” or favorable significance.
