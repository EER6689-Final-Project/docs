## Pilot interpretation and cautions
A finite browser event buffer can be evaluated as a feasibility pilot without porting a full browser instrumentation system. Calibrate event/byte generation by fixture and workload, trigger delay, buffer occupancy/overflow, persistence, and oracle agreement. Sequence IDs or equivalent continuity checks plus independent ground-truth fixture logs can expose missing records and wrong attribution.

Treat 64 MiB/session, 60 s retention, and a 2× retention-to-trigger-delay margin only as example operational choices to adapt to the machine and study. Do not present them as literature-backed constants. Likewise, capacity / average byte rate is only a rough horizon estimate for a homogeneous rolling record stream. Browser capture can have variable event sizes/rates, bursty phases, multiple buffers/processes, per-buffer loss, serialization overhead, flush delays, and triggers racing eviction. Measure empirical horizon under the worst planned fixture/workload, including bursts, and use the shortest relevant process/buffer horizon as the conservative bound.

A zero-failure result across 10 repetitions is a feasibility screen, not proof of reliability in the population. With zero failures in n independent Bernoulli trials, the one-sided 95% upper confidence bound on the failure probability is 1 - 0.05^(1/n); for n=10 this is about 25.9% (the “rule of three” approximation is 30%). Repetitions may also not be independent if they share a browser process, machine state, or fixture timing. Report trial count and uncertainty; use a fixed pilot stopping/revision rule based on any observed required-event loss, buffer warning, attribution/oracle mismatch, failure to persist, cap breach, or trigger later than measured horizon. Such a rule supports an engineering feasibility decision, not a population reliability claim.

For a pilot claim, define required evidence per failure class in advance (persistent, one-visit, replaced fault); stop/revise capture if any required record is lost, attributed to the wrong visit/action, or cannot be recovered inside the predeclared memory/time cap. Compare fresh detailed follow-up explicitly as evidence from a changed visit/state. Keep capture deadline and resource caps explicit, and scope conclusions to the selected localhost fixtures and workload.
