# Pilot-limit research receipt: finite feasibility pilot

Scope: methods literature used to guide a controlled localhost Chromium pilot of delayed browser performance diagnosis. Clinical trial sample sizes are not transferred as browser-experiment Ns. Web access was to publisher / PubMed Central full text or abstracts; some PMC HTML pages intermittently served recaptcha, so search-indexed full-text excerpts and publisher PDFs were used where noted. Checked 2026-10-31.

## Sources and supported principles

1. **Eldridge et al. (2016), “CONSORT 2010 statement: extension to randomised pilot and feasibility trials.”** DOI: [10.1186/s40814-016-0105-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40814-016-0105-8); [full text at PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5154046/); also [BMJ PDF](https://www.bmj.com/content/bmj/355/bmj.i5239.full.pdf). Access: PMC article and publisher PDF/search excerpts; publication pagination 1–9. Relevant: Box 1 / principles, p.2: pilot rationale is to investigate uncertainty about future work, feasibility aims drive methods, and a decision process should be built in; it can include criteria to proceed, amend, or stop. Checklist item 6c asks for prespecified progression criteria; item 20b asks implications for progression and proposed amendments. The extension says it does not directly cover non-randomised pilots, but principles may apply. **Transfer:** use as transparent protocol/reporting guidance, not as a browser experiment design standard.

2. **Thabane et al. (2010), “A tutorial on pilot studies: the what, why and how.”** DOI: [10.1186/1471-2288-10-1](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2288-10-1); [publisher full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1471-2288-10-1). Access: full publisher article; relevant “Sample size” section, article pages 3–4 (HTML lines 120–132). It distinguishes feasibility work from proof-of-concept/efficacy studies and recommends stating objectives and success criteria. The publisher page notes a 2023 correction to the worked numerical example; no numeric example is relied on here. **Transfer:** broad methods tutorial, not empirical browser evidence.

3. **Kunselman (2024), “A Brief Overview of Pilot Studies and Their Sample Size Justification.”** DOI: [10.1016/j.fertnstert.2024.01.040](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fertnstert.2024.01.040); [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11128343/). Access: full PMC article; “Sample Size Justification,” HTML lines 81–89; journal pages 899–901. Supports: no efficacy-style power calculation for feasibility aims; still justify N from pilot goals; prefer precision (CI) for feasibility measures; define success criteria; report uncertainty. Its medical examples/rules of thumb are not universal. **Transfer:** borrow the reasoning (aim-linked cap, descriptive estimates, uncertainty), not its numeric Ns or healthcare examples.

5. **Moore, Carter, Nietert & Stewart (2011), “Recommendations for Planning Pilot Studies in Clinical and Translational Research.”** DOI: [10.1111/j.1752-8062.2011.00347.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1752-8062.2011.00347.x); [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3203750/). Access: full PMC text, especially “Statistical analysis plan” and “Justify the Choice of Sample Size,” journal pages 332–337 (HTML lines 143–180). Supports alignment of sample size to aims, considering practical feasibility/cost/risks; small Ns can fit process-testing; focus analyses on estimates and intervals; pre-specify go/no-go rationale; CI width/precision is a valid sizing consideration when there is no hypothesis test. It cautions that N increases have diminishing precision returns. **Transfer:** strong rationale for fixed execution cap that is sized to map coverage plus practical runtime budget; not for importing its human-subject counts.

5. **Mellor et al. (2021), “Progression from external pilot to definitive randomised controlled trial: a methodological review of progression criteria reporting.”** DOI: [10.1136/bmjopen-2020-048178](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2020-048178); [publisher PDF](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/bmjopen/11/6/e048178.full.pdf). Access: full publisher PDF (12 printed pages); relevant intro p.1 and discussion pp.10–11 (PDF text lines 26–29 and 833–858). This methodological review found criteria should be prespecified; justification was infrequently reported (44/160 included publications). It cautions findings may not generalize beyond external RCT pilot reports. **Transfer:** use a traffic-light decision structure as a template; browser criteria should describe fixture validity, capture completeness, protocol completion, and resource bounds.

## Proposed finite limits for this project (judgment, not source-derived Ns)

Choose **64 planned diagnostic episodes maximum**: four protocol conditions (original-visit lightweight attribution; detailed fresh follow-up; bounded retained original evidence; periodic-rich / always-rich reference) × two fault persistence regimes (persistent and intermittent) × four distinct, independently verified fault fixtures × two repeated runs = 64. Keep each matched fixture/regime/repetition block across all four conditions so comparisons are paired; do not count repeated runs as independent causes. If the reference protocol is not intended as an experimental arm, retain it as a calibration run and state that distinction.

This cap is justified by complete coverage of the design matrix, replication to expose run-to-run instability, and a manageable fixed workload—not by a claim that 64 yields adequate power or narrow population CIs. The independent unit for generalization is the fixture/cause, only four per regime, so results remain exploratory and case-bound. Report per-cell counts, completion/missing-evidence proportions with intervals where useful, elapsed time, evidence volume, and cause-attribution outcomes descriptively. Do not make confirmatory H1 claims.

Put hard bounds in the protocol: 64 completed episodes, at most 80 starts including retries (20% retry reserve), max 15 minutes of active diagnosis per episode, and a fixed collection window of 5 working days (or a predeclared execution budget such as 16 analyst-hours). Stop at first cap reached. Retries only for preregistered infrastructure failures; retain and report failed attempts. No discretionary extension after looking at attribution outcomes.

Suggested prespecified traffic lights: **green** if all four strategies and both regimes complete with valid independent cause verification and usable evidence for at least 90% of planned episodes, within caps; **amber** if feasible after a specific repair (e.g., instrumentation/replay or capture procedure) or if 75–89% usable; **red** if fixture ground truth fails, a strategy systematically lacks enough evidence to run as specified, or fewer than 75% are usable within caps. These cut points are proposed operational rules; justify them against project tolerance, and do not call them literature-established thresholds. A safety/integrity stop (data corruption, inability to preserve ground truth, uncontrolled external traffic) overrides the planned N.

## Caveats and source hygiene

- This protocol is a controlled engineering experiment, not an RCT or clinical feasibility trial. Methodological principles transfer; healthcare thresholds, rules of thumb, and power/sample-size formulas do not.
- “Independent verified cause” should mean distinct fault-generating fixture/cause, not merely a repeated browser run. Multiple browser sessions on one cause estimate repeatability, not breadth across causes.
- These are methods papers/reviews, not evidence that the proposed numeric limits are optimal for browser diagnosis. Bibliographic correction: Moore et al. (not Leon et al.) is the author group for PMC3203750; the correct DOI and pagination are recorded above.
