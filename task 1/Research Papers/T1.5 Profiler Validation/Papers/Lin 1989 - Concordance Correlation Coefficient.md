# A concordance correlation coefficient to evaluate reproducibility

- **Citation:** Lin, L. I. (1989). A concordance correlation coefficient to evaluate reproducibility. *Biometrics, 45*(1), 255–268.
- **Zotero key:** `HB6E4DMC`
- **Source available in Zotero:** PubMed record only; no paper PDF attached. This note is based on the Zotero bibliographic record and abstract.

## Scope and contribution

Lin proposes a reproducibility index defined as the correlation of two readings relative to the 45-degree identity line. The abstract says the estimator's statistical properties can be evaluated using an inverse hyperbolic tangent transformation. It reports a Monte Carlo study with 5,000 runs and an application to actual data.

## Relevance to profiler validation

The concordance correlation coefficient (CCC) incorporates closeness to the identity line, so it addresses a different question from Pearson correlation, which measures linear association. CCC can serve as a compact supplementary summary when comparing two measurement methods.

## Evidence boundary and limitations

The abstract does not provide the simulation design, empirical application's sample or domain, estimator uncertainty values, or the operating conditions under which the index performs well. A single coefficient cannot show the distribution of per-page differences or identify local and magnitude-dependent disagreement. The paper does not validate browser-performance tools.

## Project use

If reported, use CCC alongside paired differences and distributional plots, not as a replacement for agreement limits or an error analysis tied to a justified reference.
