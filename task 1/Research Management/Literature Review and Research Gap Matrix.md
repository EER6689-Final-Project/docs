## Cross-source candidate areas (researcher inference, not novelty claims)

For competing literature, possible falsification, requirement conflicts and unresolved gates, see [[T1.1 Candidate Gap Assessment]]. The descriptions below are prompts, **not a novelty ranking**.

1. **Versioned metric conformance versus perceived usefulness.** WICG shift entries (`R2U9EAFA`) and Chrome's session-window CLS (`GYN22PP7`) are different layers; Lighthouse's versioned scoring (`WB6JZEUA`) is another. Kelton 2017, Gao 2017 and Vesper show why conformance to a browser algorithm does not automatically predict a human outcome. Candidate study: test same-artifact algorithm agreement *separately* from matched-run agreement and task-specific perceived validity. **Novelty not established** without searching existing profiler validation/metric-conformance studies.
2. **Interaction and lifecycle coverage of a synthetic run.** INP guidance (`B8XNKSHY`, `UN6NKBQX`) requires exercised inputs; CLS (`GYN22PP7`) spans page lifetime; CrUX methodology (`3KVANYXJ`, `DP9EINBS`) includes eligible field navigations with different metric denominators and SPA attribution. Mobile scrolling (Kelton 2020) and banner interactions (Wehner 2023) supply task-sensitive examples. Candidate study: predeclare which navigations/gestures a profiler observes and test missingness systematically. **Novelty not established**; this is not a claim that INP or SPA measurement lacks existing solutions.
3. **Scope-labelled diagnostics rather than a universal scalar.** Memory proposal (`NJYH4V45`) disclaims exact or cross-browser memory; old DevTools reference (`BL8XCEDY`) documents UI event categories, not a portable trace contract; SI guide (`A8GDSSI7`) describes visual capture rather than functional readiness. van Riet 2023 finds a product-specific milestone useful only in its dashboard. Candidate study: assess whether an explicit, versioned measurement contract improves reproducibility and interpretation compared with unlabeled score/diagnostic fields. **Novelty not established** and the specification corpus alone cannot validate an improvement.
4. **Population- and estimand-matched comparison.** CrUX filtering, URL folding, iframe aggregation and field navigation scope (`3KVANYXJ`, `DP9EINBS`) differ from Lighthouse's controlled score (`WB6JZEUA`) and from the Phase A recorded-video studies. Candidate study: document which cross-tool comparisons are computable on the same artifact and which require independent matched runs or field stratification. **Novelty not established**; source review of existing agreement methods is still pending.
5. **Statistical characterization of repeated live-page runs.** Systems benchmarking sources distinguish nested sources of variation and precision-driven repetition (`GJMALCNF`, `P94EL9KH`), show timing summaries can be sensitive to noise and setup (`KKG9KNEU`, `2TY5A3XC`), and treat the distribution, quantile algorithm, uncertainty, and stopping rule as separate choices (`SZBK3ZEE`, `E2A63QR8`, `TNLWEEU7`, `GANI6MKK`, `XX924A92`, `SKLNC8DZ`). Candidate study: characterize run-order, cache/session states, dependence, distribution shape, and uncertainty for the declared browser condition before fixing a headline statistic or stopping protocol. **Researcher inference, not author-stated gap or novelty claim:** most methods were evaluated on native programs, simulation, or distributed services; none sets a browser-specific run count or proves IID behavior for live navigations.

**Outstanding source work:** The W3C catalogue (`XFCVZVBE`) links individual normative specifications but does not replace reading them; the Lighthouse page explicitly labels its weight table v10, and the 2015 DevTools timeline reference may not represent current trace events. Those remain checks for a later P0 tool/specification pass. The apparent CrUX eligibility wording conflict and the two Phase A paper population/page-count issues remain flagged rather than silently reconciled.

## T1.3 Synthetic Agents and External Validity

**Status:** All 24 parent records in Zotero `Final Project Research Papers/3. Synthetic Agents` (`B2NRLIE9`) were screened. The collection contains 23 substantive external or documentary sources and one internal saved research prompt (`IL735Y87`); the prompt is recorded in the source inventory but is not evidence. Six scholarly PDFs and 18 other Zotero records have source notes, including the internal prompt. Two records duplicate T1.2 documentation rows and are cross-referenced below rather than counted again.

**Scope and interpretation:** The literature separates three questions: whether an agent executes the settings it claims (control fidelity), whether it measures a real production system/path (target realism), and whether its selected conditions represent a defined field population (population representativeness). These properties are not interchangeable. A production URL, mobile-shaped settings, or a profile informed by field data does not turn scripted measurements into RUM.

**Cross-source findings:** Field sources report performance differences by device, network, mobility, browser, page, country, and visit state within their own samples. CrUX exposes useful population strata and aggregate distributions, but its eligible Chrome sample and aggregation do not recover each user's joint device, CPU, path, state, and behavior. Browser and network documentation defines available controls; those controls alone do not validate physical-device or carrier-path equivalence. Packet emulation can express measured impairments, but the chosen trace, placement, browser, and page workload still define its scope. Repeat runs address run variability, while field comparison addresses a separate representativeness question.

**Candidate research areas (researcher inference, not novelty claims):** Evaluate device/network profiles against declared target populations and aligned metrics; test whether CPU calibration and network-shaping mechanisms reproduce target conditions; assess browser-state and navigation coverage; and compare synthetic distributions with field strata using aligned page/origin, metric/version, denominator, device class, and time window. The sources do not select a profile architecture, number or weighting of profiles, geography, state protocol, sample size, calibration tolerance, or thesis gap.
