\ReportFrontChapter{Abstract}

Repeated performance monitoring involves many visits to the same website or web application to establish baselines, detect changes and collect diagnostic evidence. However, successive visits may encounter different conditions. An expired login session may introduce authentication redirects, an uncached image may require a slow network request, or JavaScript activity may delay the display of an image that has already loaded. During follow-up profiling, the user may be authenticated, the image may be cached, or the script activity may have ended. A similar slowdown may also recur for a different reason. Repeated visits therefore do not guarantee that a later profile explains the original regression. A diagnostic reliability index is needed to quantify how consistently the original cause is identified across these monitoring episodes.

This controlled longitudinal study will compare detailed follow-up profiling with evidence retained during the original visit under matched monitoring budgets and a shared diagnosis deadline. Automated visits to controlled test websites will include independently verified resource-loading and main-thread faults that persist, disappear or are replaced by another cause. Visit identity and browser conditions will be recorded. The diagnostic reliability index will be the proportion of independently verified eligible episodes whose original cause is correctly identified within the budget and deadline, with intermittent faults forming the primary comparison. Missed alerts, failed captures, late diagnoses and abstentions will remain in the denominator. Monitoring cost, diagnostic coverage, misleading attribution and diagnosis delay will also be assessed.

A bounded feasibility pilot will precede a separately specified main experiment; neither has been conducted. The intended contribution is an evidence-based account of when delayed diagnosis remains reliable and when original-visit evidence is needed. The findings are intended to inform performance-monitoring and resource-use estimation systems that must balance monitoring cost, diagnostic accuracy and reliability, within the conditions tested.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} website performance; browser profiling; delayed diagnosis; original-visit evidence; longitudinal measurement; fault attribution
