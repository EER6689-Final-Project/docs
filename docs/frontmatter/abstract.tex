\ReportFrontChapter{Abstract}

A detailed browser profile collected after a performance alert describes a new execution. It may therefore explain the follow-up visit without identifying the cause of the original regression. This report proposes a controlled longitudinal study of that diagnostic gap. Scheduled synthetic visits to controlled website fixtures will be used to compare lightweight original-visit attribution, detailed follow-up profiling, bounded original-event capture and periodic detailed capture under common resource allowances. Always-rich capture will be assessed under a separate reference allowance. Fault activity will be checked independently on both original and follow-up visits, with persistent, transient and replaced causes distinguished. The primary comparison will be the difference in correct original-cause attribution between retained evidence and follow-up profiling for intermittent faults. All verified eligible original episodes will be included, including missed alerts, failed captures, late diagnoses, abstentions and exhausted policies. Resource use, diagnosis delay and misleading attribution will also be measured. A finite feasibility pilot with two fixtures is specified before a separate main-study protocol and analysis freeze. Neither the pilot nor the main study has been run; no experimental findings or hypothesis results are reported. The intended contribution is a bounded account of when delayed profiling can explain an earlier episode and when original-visit evidence is needed, subject to verified capture feasibility, complete cost accounting and the limits of the tested conditions.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} website performance; browser profiling; delayed diagnosis; original-visit evidence; longitudinal measurement; fault attribution
