\ReportFrontChapter{Abstract}

This report sets out a proposed study of how reliably detailed profiling during a later browser visit can identify the cause of an earlier website performance regression. The research question compares that delayed diagnosis with evidence retained during the original visit under a fixed monitoring budget. The planned design is a controlled longitudinal experiment using scheduled synthetic visits to owned or authorized website fixtures, known fault episodes, and an independent record of whether each fault was active in each visit. It will compare lightweight original-visit attribution, a rich follow-up visit, bounded original-event retention, periodic rich capture, and an always-rich reference. Correct attribution of the original fault within a common diagnosis deadline is the primary outcome; misses, failed captures, abstentions, delay, and total collection cost will also be reported. A bounded feasibility pilot is proposed before a separately frozen main-study protocol. No pilot or main-study observations are reported here, and the hypotheses remain untested. The intended contribution is a measured account of when a later visit can support diagnosis of an earlier episode, and when that inference is limited by fault persistence, delay, or browser state. Results, if collected under the approved protocol, will be limited to the tested fixtures and conditions.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} website performance; browser profiling; delayed diagnosis; original-visit evidence; longitudinal measurement; fault attribution
