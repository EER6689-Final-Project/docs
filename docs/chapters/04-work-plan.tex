\section{Sequence and Dependencies}

The research will proceed from measurement and fault verification to a bounded pilot, followed by a separate main-study decision. Collection will depend on verified host capacity, fixed software and fixture versions, isolated evaluator labels, qualified capture contracts and enforceable resource limits. Main-study collection will additionally depend on a reviewed protocol that defines the target population, allocation, outcomes, analysis and stopping rule.

Table~\ref{tab:work-sequence} gives relative stages rather than unconfirmed calendar dates. The person-day ranges are provisional planning estimates. FP-0.1's seven-calendar-day and eight-active-hour limits are protocol caps, distinct from those effort estimates. The researcher will be responsible for implementation, collection, records and analysis. The main protocol and progression decision will require review; the reviewer has not yet been assigned.

The stages have scientific dependencies. Capture contracts cannot be qualified without a reproducible fixture and independent execution check. Strategy costs cannot be compared without both the physical campaign ledger and the standalone policy ledgers. A main-study sample cannot be justified by the pilot visit count alone; variation, dependence, independent fixture coverage and a practical effect or precision target must first be considered \parencite{GJMALCNF,E8MGSAC8}.

\section{Resources and Deliverables}

The initial resources will be a localhost test environment, a host able to enforce the proposed CPU and memory limits, one fixed Chromium and automation stack, isolated evaluator storage, access logs and a watchdog for time, memory and storage limits. Resource availability will be verified before the schedule begins. The pilot's caps will limit feasibility work; any later main-study budget will be set from measured costs and the chosen design.

The research deliverables will be a versioned measurement protocol and data dictionary; reproducible fixtures and independent fault checks; a longitudinal episode dataset and complete attempt ledger; capture configurations and cost records for A--E; conformance and perturbation evidence; and a comparison of diagnostic outcomes and reliability conditions. A stopped or amended pilot will still produce an auditable record of what was attempted and why the protocol could not proceed.

The related engineering work will provide the browser profiler service/API and seven performance views described in Chapter~\ref{chap:introduction}. It will include metric, network, main-thread, memory and timeline outputs, reference-tool comparisons and the specified unit tests. This delivery can use the research contracts and provenance structure, but its broader feature coverage will remain separate from the pilot's LCP-based causal comparison. Reference-tool agreement will be reported as measurement consistency, not independent proof of the original fault.

\section{Progression, Change Control and Risks}

The progression rules in Section~\ref{sec:pilot-main} will determine whether the pilot version is stopped, amended or used to prepare the main protocol. A green outcome will indicate only that further study design is justified. An amber outcome will require a specific prospective amendment. The one permitted pre-comparison repair cycle will remain within its 48-start reserve and all campaign caps. A change to the fault class, fixture, detector, capture mechanism or resource allocation will require a new protocol version.

After comparison begins, outcomes will not be used to adjust that same comparison. Detected arm-specific loss or timeout will remain an outcome, while shared record-integrity failures will end the version. Any later change to the main protocol will be documented and reviewed before the affected collection. This sequence preserves the distinction between feasibility learning and confirmatory evaluation.
